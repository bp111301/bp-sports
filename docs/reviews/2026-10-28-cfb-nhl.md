Monday–Sunday cohorts were 39–19, 38–21, 33–23 and 1–1. The latest week is only two completed games. Do not compare whole-season historical development accuracy directly with this October-heavy, mostly conference-game cohort.

## 3. CFB Candidate B: probability improvement, no winner upgrade

Candidate B changes the selected winner in only **2/175 games**: it fixes one V1 miss and introduces one different miss. Net accuracy improvement: **zero**.

Its paired Brier improvement is 0.00136 and log-loss improvement 0.00436. These are small:
- Accuracy difference, paired day-block 95% interval: **−1.47 to +1.59 percentage points**.
- Brier difference (candidate minus V1), paired-game interval: −0.00275 to +0.00003; paired-day interval: −0.00279 to −0.00043.
- Log-loss difference, paired-day interval: −0.00777 to −0.00172.

The uncertainty method matters, and the changes are not a demonstrated winner-selection upgrade. Both models share the main confidence weakness. **Continue Candidate B's existing shadow collection; do not promote it as an accuracy fix.**

## 4. NHL: modest signal, wide uncertainty

The reference's mean confidence is **59.33%**, close to its **58.71%** realized accuracy. Its overall calibration gap is much smaller than CFB's, although that does not establish calibration of every probability band.

Most NHL reference picks are close:
- 50–60% band: **49–44**, averaging 54.4% confidence.
- 60–70% band: **33–18**, averaging 64.5%.
- Only **11** reference games exceed 70%; there is insufficient evidence for a reliable high-confidence shortlist.

The recency model improves four decisions net: 10 challenger-only correct and six control-only correct across 16 disagreements. Its +2.58-point accuracy difference has a paired day-block 95% interval of **−2.92 to +7.69 points**. Brier and log-loss difference intervals also include zero.

The confirmed-goalie model improves three decisions net: five fixes and two new misses across seven disagreements. Its +2.26-point accuracy difference has a paired day-block interval of **−2.04 to +6.34 points**; probability-metric intervals include zero too.

**Neither result establishes a reliable gain yet.** All NHL candidates remain research only; their historical release gate is still unmet. Do not bypass that gate because a short live sample looks better.

## 5. NHL input-treatment concern

The existing [Seattle audit](https://github.com/bp111301/bp-sports/blob/1b58a6c802c525f2659b38a16a71cc6970365d0e/research/nhl/audits/SEATTLE_20261006.md) independently reproduced the saved 92.36% reference probability and identified sensitivity to an isolated missing power-play feature. The missingness indicator multiplied home-win odds by roughly 18.8 in that diagnostic snapshot.

In this review's paired snapshots:
- Power-play difference missing: 13 games, **7–6**, Brier 0.29417.
- Power-play present: 120 games, **74–46**, Brier 0.22848.
- Only **one** game has an isolated missing power-play difference with all other features present: that same Seattle example.

These small, different cohorts do not prove all power-play missingness is harmful. The known sensitivity is a reason to build a separate, historically evaluated missingness-robust candidate. **Do not disable an indicator, cap probabilities, or alter the frozen reference in place.** The exact original morning source rows were not archived; zero power-play opportunities are a plausible missingness mechanism, not a verified cause of that original capture.

## 6. Baselines and what we cannot claim

On the same games, always selecting the home team would have gone:
- CFB: **104–71 (59.4%)**, versus V1's 111–64: seven additional correct picks.
- NHL: **88–67 (56.8%)**, versus reference's 91–64: three additional correct picks.

A fixed 55% home-win probability has Brier 0.24307 / log loss 0.67925 for CFB and 0.24573 / 0.68458 for NHL. Both primary models improve the point estimates slightly, but paired uncertainty intervals include no gain. The 55% probability is an illustrative heuristic, not a fitted or previously registered benchmark.

No sufficiently complete archived, as-of market-favorite baseline was established for these exact games. This review therefore **does not establish that either model beats market favorites or has a betting edge**. Market data should stay in a separate comparison layer.

## 7. Concrete next steps

1. **CFB input observability and diagnosis first.** Archive separate append-only input receipts, complete as-of features and component probabilities. Investigate major-conference/away-selection weaknesses with pre-2026 walk-forward data.
2. **Separate probability and winner-quality research.** Develop a historically calibrated CFB challenger and a separately tested winner-selection hypothesis. Calibration alone generally changes probability quality, not which team is selected.
3. **NHL missingness robustness.** Prepare a new version with tested early-season feature handling; keep the current watchlist and goalie pairs frozen.
4. **Continue same-game testing; promote nothing today.** The proposed next candidate protocol, sample checkpoints and release conditions are in [research_plan.md](../../research/prospective_review/20261028/research_plan.md). No new trained bundle or candidate forecasts were created in this review.

Do not fit a fix on these inspected October games and call performance on those same games a fresh test. Do not retroactively select a confidence band to advertise 75–80% accuracy.

## Method, reproducibility and limits

- Inputs pinned to main **2ff8cf28a5156625fc86094c5f45b3811708f881** and NHL research **1b58a6c802c525f2659b38a16a71cc6970365d0e**.
- Scores use the pinned feed; independent schedule extracts were fetched separately and restricted to games due by the fixed cutoff. Ongoing/unsettled games are excluded from accuracy. Every report denominator is its eligible settled cohort.
- Wilson intervals describe binomial accuracy uncertainty. Paired comparisons use 10,000 deterministic resamples of identical games and of calendar-day blocks. Day-block resampling accounts for some same-day dependence, not all repeated-team or cross-day dependence.
- CFB has only three complete prospective weeks, despite 175 games. Small subgroups, multiple inspected slices, candidate selection and repeated-team dependencies limit conclusions. There is no causal attribution from outcome splits alone.
- Brier/log loss use original probabilities, not current reruns. The code does no fitting, bundle loading, forecast generation, or settlement updates.
- Inputs are hash-checked after canonical UTF-8 final-newline normalization. Results and compact independent-source evidence are saved alongside the code. The larger primary ledgers remain at their original pinned GitHub locations.
- Five focused analysis checks cover probability metrics, excluding ineligible rows, identical-game pairing, mismatched outcome rejection, duplicate pairing rejection, and timing/label audit detection.
